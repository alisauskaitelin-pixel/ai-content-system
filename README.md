# ai-content-system

The system I run a brand's weekly content with. It takes a week of events and turns it into a finished content calendar with the copy written for six channels (X, Medium, email, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok), each piece checked for AI-sounding writing before I see it. Built in Claude Code, running since April 2026.

I built it because I was the only marketer on a product with six channels to feed, and most of my week went into first drafts & back-and-forth on copy. Now the system drafts, checks and rewrites, and my part is the calls only a person should make: what the week is about, what's off, what ships. By my own count it took my weekly content work from 2-3 hours to about 40 minutes.

The volume was never the point. I wanted the work off one person without the quality sliding, so nothing ships that I wouldn't put my name on.

---

## What makes it more than a prompt

- **Memory it reads every run.** Product facts, how each channel has performed, who the audience is, a bank of evergreen posts for slow weeks. Planning starts from what's true and what has worked.
- **A rules layer.** Banned words and patterns, a format contract per channel, and a file of every post a human has approved, which new copy gets measured against.
- **One skill per channel.** Each carries its own opener, structure, length and voice, and loads before drafting for that channel.
- **A learnings loop.** Every correction and every win gets written back to memory, so the same mistake doesn't come back next week and approved copy becomes the new reference.

---

## A week, step by step

Every step ends with something I approve before the next one starts.

1. **Intake.** What's happening, what's launching, any constraints. No drafting yet.
2. **Context.** Memory loads: product facts, last week's best post & why it worked, the audience.
3. **Performance.** If last week's numbers are in, the system reads them and makes one recommendation for this week.
4. **The week's story.** Two or three sentences on what the week is, who it's for and what they should feel by Sunday.
5. **Channel map.** Every day across six channels, what feeds what ("the trailer cuts into a Reel"). Still no copy.
6. **Angle pass.** Before any copy, each piece gets the one angle it leads with, the most charged thing in it. This is where the descriptive recap dies, because by drafting time it has already won.
7. **Draft.** Full copy per channel, through that channel's contract, one idea per post.
8. **Visual briefs** for every post that needs one.
9. **Copy check.** Everything gets scored by [copy-check](https://github.com/alisauskaitelin-pixel/copy-check). Below 75, it's rewritten (up to three times) before I see it.
10. **Algorithm pass.** No two posts the same shape in a day, no overposting, nothing a platform's own quality filter would read as press release.
11. **My review.** I flag specific pieces with a reason. If my note conflicts with a rule, the system says so instead of quietly picking one.
12. **Ship & learn.** Approved posts go into the scheduling tool, and the session's corrections go back into memory.

---

## Why it holds

The angle pass and the copy check do most of the work. Every content system I've seen fails the same way, by describing what happened, and the only place to stop that is in planning, before a word is written. The copy check then keeps anything that reads machine-made off my desk.

---

## What's not here

The brand's memory files, its approved copy and its numbers. The structure is the part that's shareable, and the part that carries over to any brand.
